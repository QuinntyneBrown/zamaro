Pattern

Notifications

Notifications tell a church what just happened or what changed while they were away. Toasts confirm and fade, banners stay until the church acts, and e-mail carries anything that matters after the tab is closed.

Overview

A booking takes days: Naomi sends a request on Friday, Abigail answers on Sunday, the deposit is paid on Monday, and the service is five weeks later. So most news arrives when Naomi is not on the page. Zamaro uses three channels, chosen by how much the news needs from her:

  • Toast: “Saved Luz Viva”. A confirmation of something she just did. It fades on its own; errors stay.
  • Banner: “Abigail accepted Sat 14 Nov. Pay the $162.50 deposit to lock it in.” News that needs an action, shown on every page under the top bar until it is done.
  • E-mail: “Abigail Mensah accepted your request for Sat 14 Nov”. Anything that matters if the tab is closed: accepted, declined, receipts, the end of free cancellation.

A notification centre (a bell with a history) is not in the first release. Until then “Your requests” is the history, and the top bar shows a count when something needs action.

Composes: toast, alert & banner, badge, top bar, button, card, description list and save toggle.

Only one banner shows at a time: the highest priority. The rest wait in “Your requests”.

Auto-dismiss and grouping

  • Success and info toasts stay at least 8 seconds, plus 2 seconds when they carry an action such as Undo (WCAG 2.2.1).
  • The timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast, while anything inside it has focus, and while the window is in the background.
  • Error toasts never auto-dismiss. Neither do banners.
  • At most three toasts at once, newest at the bottom. A fourth pushes the oldest success toast out early; errors are never pushed out.
  • Repeats collapse: saving three artists in a row shows one toast, “Saved 3 artists”, with Undo for the last.
  • While a dialog is open, new toasts wait until it closes.

Responsive behaviour

  • Toasts sit bottom right, 24rem wide, 24px from the edges from 640px. On phones they span the width 16px from the edges and sit above the safe area. They stack upwards.
  • Banners run the full width under the sticky top bar and scroll away with the page. The text wraps; the action wraps under it on phones.
  • A toast must never hide the focused control (WCAG 2.4.11). On phones, while the booking stub’s submit has focus, toasts wait.
  • E-mails are one column, 36rem at most, with a full-width button on phones.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Two toast regions are in the page from the start: a polite role="status" region for confirmations and a role="alert" region for errors. Adding a toast to a region announces it; a region created at the same moment as its toast is often missed. Banners use role="status", or role="alert" for priority 1. See the APG Alert pattern.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabBanners are in the page order straight after the top bar. Toasts sit at the end of the page; the action they offer is always available elsewhere too (the toggle undoes a save).
Enter / SpaceRuns Undo, Try again or the banner action.
EscDismisses the toast that has focus and returns focus to the control that caused it.

Focus

Notifications never take focus when they appear. Focusing inside a toast pauses its timer. Dismissing a banner moves focus to the start of <main>; dismissing a toast returns focus to its trigger.

Labelling

Dismiss buttons name what they dismiss: “Dismiss: Saved Luz Viva”, “Hide until tomorrow”. Count badges carry their meaning: “3 saved artists”, “1 needs action”. The toast title is the announcement, so it stands on its own.

Motion

Toasts rise 16px and fade in over --duration-slow, and slide right as they leave over --duration-base. Under reduced motion they appear and disappear at once. Banners do not animate.

Touch

Toast and banner buttons are 36px tall inside 16px of padding, so their targets reach 44px. Dismiss buttons are 36px squares at the edge. Nothing needs a swipe; swipe-to-dismiss is an extra, never the only way.

Content

  • Toast title: past-tense verb and the name: “Saved Luz Viva”, “Removed Elijah Park”, “Request sent to Abigail”, “Deposit paid · Sat 14 Nov is locked in”.
  • Error toast: “Couldn’t {verb} {name}” plus what is fine: “Your other saved artists are fine.” and Try again.
  • Banner: the news in bold, then the action and the money or the date: “Abigail accepted Sat 14 Nov. Pay the $162.50 deposit to lock it in.”
  • E-mail subject: who did what, for which date: “Abigail Mensah accepted your request for Sat 14 Nov”. No “Update on your booking”.
  • No exclamation marks, no “Success!”, no “Congratulations”. Warm, not loud.
