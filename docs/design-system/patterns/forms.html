Pattern

Forms

Forms are how a church asks for a date: the search on the poster and the booking request on the ticket stub. Labels sit above the field, errors wait until you leave it, and the submit button says what it is doing.

Overview

The mocks have two forms. The search is the booking bar on the poster: four fields with sensible defaults and one submit, “Show the lineup”. The booking request is the stub beside an artist’s profile: date, kind of gathering, church and an optional message, sent with “Request to book · Sat 14 Nov”. Every future form (the artist application, church details, cancelling a booking) follows the same rules.

  • One column, labels above. People read down the stub the way they read a ticket. Two columns appear only for short related fields, and only from 768px up.
  • Validate late, re-check early. A field is checked when the person leaves it. Once it shows an error, it is re-checked on every keystroke so the error disappears the moment it is fixed.
  • On submit, collect every problem. An error summary at the top of the form takes focus and links to each field.
  • Say what is happening. The submit button stays in place and changes its label (“Sending request…”). Success replaces the form with what happens next.

Composes: form field, text field, select, date picker, textarea, checkbox, form layout, inline message, alert, button and the booking ticket.

When to use

Pick the form shape from what the person is doing, not from how many fields there are.

MomentWhat happensWhy

<form class="stub" novalidate aria-labelledby="book-title">
  <div class="stub__head"><h2 id="book-title" class="stub__title">Book Abigail</h2><span class="stub__code" aria-hidden="true">No. ZAM-0114</span></div>
  <div class="field">
    <label class="field__label" for="date">Event date</label>
    <input class="input" id="date" type="date" value="2026-11-14" aria-describedby="date-msg">
    <span class="inline-msg inline-msg--success" id="date-msg"><svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" …/>Abigail is free Sat 14 Nov</span>
  </div>
  …
  <button class="btn btn--primary btn--lg btn--block" type="submit">Request to book · Sat 14 Nov</button>
  <p class="stub__fine">Nothing is charged until Abigail accepts. Cancel free up to 14 days before.</p>
</form>
  1. Form label. The stub title names the form (“Book Abigail”) through aria-labelledby. The booking number is decoration for sighted readers.
  2. Field. .field stacks label, control, then help or error, 4px apart. Fields are 20px apart (--space-5).
  3. Label above. .field__label in the stamp style. Optional fields say “Optional” with .field__optional; required fields carry no marker because nearly every field is required.
  4. Help, status and error. One line under the control, linked with aria-describedby. Help is muted, status uses .inline-msg with an icon, errors use .field__error.
  5. Submit. The one yellow button. It names the action and the date, and fills the stub.
  6. Fine print. .stub__fine says what the submit costs and what happens next.

Responsive behaviour

  • Booking bar: one column below 640px, two columns from 640px (--layout-breakpoint-sm). The title and the submit always span the full row.
  • Booking stub: from 1024px it sits in the --layout-sidebar-width (22rem) column and stays pinned while the profile scrolls. Below 1024px it follows the reviews, and “Book for Sat 14 Nov” in the poster jumps to it.
  • Long forms: one column below 768px. .form__row puts two short related fields side by side from 768px only (first and last name, city and postal code). .form__actions wraps, primary first.
  • Submit: full width inside the stub and on phones (.btn--block); the label must fit at 360px, so “Request to book · Sat 14 Nov” is the longest allowed.
  • Error summary: when it takes focus, the page scrolls it below the sticky top bar (scroll-padding-top is the top bar height plus 16px). Summary links scroll the field into view with its label visible.
  • Native pickers: the date and selects use native controls, so phones show their own wheel or calendar and the field never overflows.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <form> named by its heading (aria-labelledby), with novalidate so our messages replace the browser’s bubbles. Native inputs, selects and textareas only. There is no APG pattern for forms; the W3C Forms tutorial covers labels, grouping, notifications and multi-page forms.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves between fields in visual order, then to the submit. Leaving a field runs its check.
EnterIn a text field, submits the form. In a textarea, adds a new line.
↑ ↓ / SpaceChange a select or open its list; step the date parts in a date field.
Enter on a summary linkMoves focus to the field with the problem.

Focus

Focus never moves while someone types. On a failed submit, focus moves to the error summary (tabindex="-1"), which is announced as an alert. On success, focus moves to the success title so the screen reader reads “Request sent to Abigail”. While busy, focus stays on the submit button.

Labelling

  • Every control has a visible label above it, tied with <label for>. A placeholder is only a hint and disappears when typing.
  • Help, status and errors sit outside the label and are linked with aria-describedby. Inside a wrapping label they would become part of the field’s name.
  • aria-invalid="true" goes on the control with the error; remove it the moment the value is fixed.
  • Name, organization and town fields use autocomplete (organization, address-level2) so browsers can fill them (WCAG 1.3.5).
  • Errors use colour, an icon and bold text, never colour alone (1.4.1).

Motion

Nothing in a form moves except the submit spinner and the field focus ring. Errors appear without animation so the layout settles at once. The spinner keeps turning under reduced motion because it is a status indicator.

Touch

Controls are 44px tall (--control-height-md) and the stub submit 56px. Fields are 20px apart, so a mis-tap lands on nothing rather than on the next field. Summary links are full sentences, easy to hit.

Content

  • Labels are short nouns or a plain question, in sentence case: “Event date”, “Kind of gathering”, “How far can they drive?”.
  • Placeholders give an example, never an instruction: “Your church, the room, the songs you love.”
  • Errors say what is wrong and how to fix it, in one sentence, naming the field: “Enter your church’s name so Abigail knows who’s asking.” Never “Invalid input” or “This field is required”.
  • Summary title counts the problems: “2 things to fix”. Each link is the start of the field error.
  • Submit is a verb and, where useful, the date: “Show the lineup”, “Request to book · Sat 14 Nov”. Busy labels use the present tense: “Checking calendars…”, “Sending request…”.
  • Fine print under the submit says what it costs: “Nothing is charged until Abigail accepts.”
  • Success names the person and the next step: “Request sent to Abigail. We’ll e-mail you when she answers.”

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • Field help “✓ Abigail is free Sat 14 Nov” was plain muted text with a typed ✓ inside the label. It is now an .inline-msg--success with an icon, outside the label.
  • No field error element existed. It is now .field__error with aria-invalid and aria-describedby, and invalid styling covers input, select and textarea instead of only .input.
  • The stub heading used style="font: var(--text-h3)". It is now .stub__title.
  • The sort field used style="min-width: 12rem" in four mocks. It is now .field--inline.
