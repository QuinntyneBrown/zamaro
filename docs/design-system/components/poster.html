Poster hero

The poster is the charcoal stage at the top of the page. It sets the question as a tour date (“Who’s free Sat 14 Nov”) or the artist’s name as a headliner, with a screen-printed halftone in the corner.

Overview

Every page opens with one poster. It holds the page’s only <h1> and is always on the stage (.on-stage), so it stays charcoal in both themes. Two layouts share the stage:

  • Search poster (.poster__grid) on Discover: a kicker, the question with the date set huge, one line of context, and the booking bar beside it on desktop.
  • Artist poster (.artist-poster) on a profile: the artist’s name at poster size, up to four facts, the actions (“Book for Sat 14 Nov”, Save, Share) and the portrait as artwork.

Use it once per page, at the top, directly under the top bar. Do not use it for in-page sections: those use .section with a .section__head. Do not use it to report errors in results. Discover keeps its poster unchanged and shows an alert in the lineup.

Anatomy

  1. Kicker.overline.poster__kicker: mono 12px, 0.18em tracking, yellow on the stage. Says where or who in under eight words.
  2. Title.poster__title: condensed display, --text-h1 (48–88px), uppercase from CSS. The page <h1>.
  3. Date.poster__date: --text-display (64–160px), yellow. Each word is its own no-wrap span so “14 Nov” never splits.
  4. Sub line.poster__sub: 18px body on --color-fg-on-stage-muted. One sentence with a count and a place.
  5. Halftone corner.poster::after: yellow dots at 35% opacity fading out from the bottom right. Decorative, never read aloud.
  6. Stage.on-stage: --color-bg-stage with paper text in both themes. Padding 48/64px, 64/80px from 992px.

Variants

Choose by page: the search poster asks a question and carries the search form; the artist poster names one artist and carries their actions. Specimens on this page scale the display type down to fit the docs column; in the product the date and name use the full --text-display and --text-poster sizes.

Check dates
Without a rating, the yellow fact says “New” and the second says “No reviews yet”. Opened without a search date, the Book button reads “Check dates” (L2-012). The portrait uses the plain halftone, not yellow, which is kept for headliners.

Sizes

The poster has one size. It always spans the full width, and its type scales with the viewport through clamp() tokens.

States

The poster has no pointer states of its own: its buttons, links and fields carry those. What changes is the copy and, while the profile loads, the whole artist header. Discover keeps the same poster when results fail, because the failure belongs to the lineup, not the search.

Responsive behaviour

  • Padding: 48px top and 64px bottom on phones, 64px and 80px from --layout-breakpoint-lg (992px). Side margins come from .container (--layout-margin: 20, 40, then 64px).
  • Search poster: copy and booking bar stack below 992px. From 992px .poster__grid becomes two columns (1.15fr and 1fr), bottom-aligned, 64px apart.
  • Date: --text-display is clamp(4rem, 15vw, 10rem). Words wrap as whole units, so “Sat” can drop above “14 Nov” but the day and month never split. At 360px “14 Nov” is about 64px tall and fits.
  • Artist poster: portrait first and capped at 18rem on phones; from 768px it moves into a 15rem right column (20rem from 992px), bottom-aligned with the name. The name breaks anywhere (overflow-wrap: anywhere) instead of scrolling sideways, which matters for “Grace Tabernacle Mass Choir”.
  • Halftone corner: fixed at 22rem and clipped by the poster’s overflow: hidden, so it never causes horizontal scroll.

Theming

The poster sits on the stage in both themes. .on-stage (shared with the top bar and footer) sets --color-bg-stage and --color-fg-on-stage; the kicker, date and yellow fact read --color-accent-on-stage; the sub line and facts read --color-fg-on-stage-muted. In light the stage is charcoal on newsprint; in dark it drops to the deepest ink so it still reads as a stage above the dark canvas.

The poster has no component tokens. Re-skin it by overriding the stage tokens on .on-stage. Children follow automatically: secondary buttons turn into paper outlines, the focus ring and link hover turn yellow, skeletons use --color-bg-stage-raised, and the booking bar opts out with data-theme="light".

/* The screen-print corner: yellow dots, faded out with a mask */
.poster::after { background: radial-gradient(circle, var(--color-accent-on-stage) 22%, transparent 24%) 0 0 / 0.85rem 0.85rem; opacity: 0.35; }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <section> labelled by its heading (aria-labelledby), which makes it a named region. It holds the page <h1>; no other heading on the page is level one. There is no APG pattern: it is static content. The portrait is role="img" with an aria-label that describes the photo; the halftone corner is a pseudo-element and is never announced.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the breadcrumb, the actions and the booking-bar fields in reading order. The poster itself is not focusable.
EnterFollows “Book for Sat 14 Nov” to the booking stub. The target heading takes tabindex="-1" so focus moves with the scroll.

Focus

On the stage the 3px ring turns yellow (--color-accent-on-stage), well above 3:1 against charcoal. Inside the paper booking bar the ring goes back to ink.

Labelling

The title is read as one phrase: “Who’s free Sat 14 Nov”. CSS adds the uppercase, so screen readers do not spell it out. Facts are separate spans, so they are read as a list of short phrases. While loading, the visually hidden <h1> stays a heading and a separate role="status" line announces the load. Never put role="status" on the <h1> itself, because it removes the heading role.

Motion

Nothing on the poster moves. Loading skeletons shimmer on --duration-deliberate and stop under prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

Actions are 44px buttons 8px apart. Breadcrumb links are at least 24px tall. The poster has no hover-only content.

Content

  • Kicker: where or who, under eight words. “Worship artists within driving distance of Toronto”, “Headliner · Gospel & contemporary vocalist”.
  • Title: the question the page answers, then the date in short form: “Who’s free Sat 14 Nov”. Write it in sentence case; CSS uppercases it.
  • Sub line: one sentence with a count and a place. “Seven artists are free that Saturday and will drive to Burlington.” While loading, say what is happening and end with an ellipsis.
  • Artist name: exactly as the artist writes it, with “&” for duos (“Daniel & Ruth Okonkwo”).
  • Facts: at most four, the rating first: “★ 4.9 · 38 churches · Brampton, ON · Drives up to 120 km”. A new artist shows “New · No reviews yet”.
  • Errors: show language in the kicker (“Show postponed”), plain words in the title (“This profile didn’t load”). The reason and the fix go in the alert below.

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • The halftone mask read the primitive --palette-ink. It now uses currentColor, since a mask only needs an opaque colour.
  • Loading skeletons were sized with inline style widths, heights and aspect ratios. They now use .skeleton--short, --medium, --portrait and .artist-poster > .skeleton caps the portrait at 18rem.
  • Stage skeletons read --palette-ink-800/700. They now read --color-bg-stage-raised and --color-bg-stage-raised-hover.
  • The artist error page padded its alert with an inline padding-block. It now uses .page-error.
  • The booking bar in the poster read paper and ink primitives to stay light on the stage. It is now a data-theme="light" island (see Booking ticket).
  • artist/loading put role="status" on the <h1>. The mock now keeps a visually hidden <h1> and a separate status line, as this page documents.
