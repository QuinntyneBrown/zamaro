Footer

The footer closes every page on the charcoal stage, like the bottom of a gig poster: the Zamaro word set huge in yellow, then short columns of links for churches, artists and the signed-in booker.

Overview

Every page ends with the footer. It repeats the routes people look for at the bottom of a page (how booking works, how to join as an artist, how to reach Zamaro) and gives a signed-in booker a shortcut to their saved artists. It is the page’s <footer> landmark.

  • Full (.footer.on-stage): the yellow word, a one-line description and three link columns. Use it on public pages.
  • Simple (.footer--simple): one short row. Use it where the person is in the middle of a task, such as the booking flow or the artist dashboard, so the footer does not compete.

Do not put primary actions in the footer. “Request to book” and “Apply as an artist” belong in the page (the booking ticket and the band).

Anatomy

  1. Stage and rule--color-bg-stage with a 4px yellow rule along the top, mirroring the top bar.
  2. The word.footer__word: “Zamaro” in the display face at --text-display, yellow. Decorative (aria-hidden).
  3. DescriptionOne sentence on what Zamaro is, in body text.
  4. Column headingAn <h2> in the mono overline style. It names the audience: churches, artists, the booker.
  5. LinksUnderlined, inheriting paper text; yellow on hover. 12px between items.
  6. Account columnOnly when signed in: the booker’s name, church and “Saved artists (3)”.

Responsive behaviour

  • Below 768px: one column. The word and description, then each link group stacked, 32px apart.
  • 768–1023px: the word spans the full width; the three link groups sit in three columns under it.
  • 1024px and wider: four columns, 2fr for the word and 1fr for each group.
  • The word scales with the viewport (--font-size-5xl is clamp(4rem, 15vw, 10rem)). With the condensed display face it fits its column; if the fallback face is wider, it can run past its column, so keep the word to one short line.
  • Bottom padding is --space-24 so the last link clears the toast region and the phone’s home indicator.

Theming

The footer is a stage surface in both themes: .footer shares the .on-stage rule, reading --color-bg-stage and --color-fg-on-stage. The word and link hover use --color-accent-on-stage; the top rule uses --color-accent. On the dark theme the stage drops to the darkest charcoal so the footer still reads as a separate band below the dark canvas.

There are no component tokens. The footer’s links are re-skinned by the shared stage rule :is(.on-stage, .topbar) a:not(.btn), the same one the top bar uses.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <footer> that is a direct child of <body>, so it is the page’s contentinfo landmark. Link groups are lists under <h2> headings, so screen-reader users can jump between them by heading. No APG widget pattern applies; it is plain links.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the links in reading order: column by column, top to bottom.
EnterFollows the link (or opens the mail app for hello@zamaro.ca).

Focus

The ring is yellow on the stage, 3px with a 3px offset. Nothing in the footer traps or moves focus.

Labelling

The giant word is aria-hidden="true": it repeats the brand that the top bar already names, and reading “Zamaro” once more adds nothing. Column headings name their audience. The mail link shows the address itself, so its purpose is clear out of context (WCAG 2.4.4).

Motion

None. Link colour changes instantly.

Touch

Footer links are inline text in a list with 12px between items, so each line is a target at least 24px tall (WCAG 2.5.8). On phones the column layout gives every link its own full-width row.

Content

  • Column headings name people, not site sections: “Churches”, “Artists”, then the booker’s own name.
  • Links start with a verb or say exactly where they go: “Find who’s free”, “Join the lineup”, “What artists earn”.
  • Two or three links per column. The footer is a short list of routes, not a site map.
  • The description is one sentence: “Worship artists for churches around Toronto.”
  • Counts match the top bar: “Saved artists (3)”.
