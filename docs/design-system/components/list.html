List

A list stacks short records that are read one at a time: Naomi’s saved artists, her requests on a phone, the links in her account.

Overview

Use a list for a handful of records with one title and one line of detail each. People read down the list and pick one; they don’t compare columns.

  • Static lists show facts: who is on the bill, what a booking includes.
  • Interactive lists (.list--interactive) make each whole row a link or button: open a saved artist, switch between requests.
  • Use a table when people compare three or more facts across rows, and ticket cards when they browse artists with pictures and prices.
  • The setlist and tour dates are specialised lists with their own pages.

Anatomy

  1. Leading avatar or icon (optional)A 40px avatar. Hidden from assistive technology when the name sits beside it.
  2. Title.list__title: bold sans 14px. The name of the record.
  3. Meta.list__meta: one line of detail in muted 14px, parts separated by middle dots.
  4. Trail (optional).list__trail: pushed to the end; a badge, a price or, in static lists only, a small button.
  5. Divider.list--divided: a hairline between items, not above the first.

Variants

Start with the simplest list that carries the content. Add an avatar only when it helps people recognise the record, and dividers only when items have two lines.

Sizes

Lists have one size. Height follows the content: one line, two lines, or a 40px avatar.

States

Static items have no states. Interactive items react to the pointer, keyboard focus and being the open item. Hover and focus are forced with data-state.

Responsive behaviour

  • Items are a single flex row at every width. The content column shrinks and wraps (min-width: 0); the avatar and the trail never shrink.
  • At 360px a badge trail such as “Free Sat 14 Nov” still fits beside a two-line title. If a trail is longer than about 16 characters, move it into the meta line instead.
  • Below --layout-breakpoint-md (768px) a short table can turn into an interactive list: the first column becomes the title, the next one or two become the meta line, the status becomes the trail.
  • In a side panel the list fills the panel’s width; it has no width of its own.

Theming

The list has no component tokens: it reads semantic tokens directly. Text is --color-fg-default and --color-fg-muted, dividers are --color-border-default, hover is --color-accent-subtle, and the current item is the inverse pair --color-bg-inverse / --color-fg-inverse. Avatars inside bring their own tokens (see Avatar).

In the dark theme the highlighter turns deep amber and the current item flips to a paper fill with charcoal text, so “current” still reads as the strongest row on the stage.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A native <ul> (or <ol> when order matters) with role="list", because list-style: none makes Safari drop list semantics. Interactive rows are ordinary links or buttons inside each <li>; this is not the APG Listbox pattern, so there is no arrow-key selection. A list of navigation links sits in a <nav> with an aria-label. For links the current item uses aria-current="true" (or "page" when the link is the page itself).

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves from one interactive row to the next. Static lists are skipped.
EnterFollows the row’s link or activates its button.
SpaceActivates a row that is a <button>; scrolls the page on a link.

Focus

The ring wraps the whole .list__link, 3px with a 3px offset, so it stays visible on the ink current item. Opening an item moves focus to the opened content’s heading; going back returns focus to the same row.

Labelling

The row’s accessible name is its text: title, meta and trail, in that order (“Abigail Mensah Sat 14 Nov · Worship night Requested”). Keep it short for that reason. Avatars next to a name are aria-hidden="true". Label the list itself when there is more than one on the page (“Naomi’s saved artists”).

Dividers are decorative and exempt from 1.4.11.

Motion

The hover fill changes instantly; nothing moves. Loading skeletons stop shimmering under prefers-reduced-motion.

Touch

Each .list__link is at least 44px tall (--target-comfortable) and spans the full width, so the whole row is the target. Rows have no gap between them; the divider marks the boundary.

Content

  • The title is the name people scan for: the artist, the church, the gathering. Don’t start it with a status or a date.
  • Meta is one line, most useful first, joined with middle dots: “Brampton · 44 km · from $650”.
  • Trails are short: one badge, one price or one small button. If you need two, the record wants a table or a card.
  • Sort by what people look for: saved artists by distance, requests by date.
