Artwork

Every artist image is printed like a gig poster: a halftone dot screen with a silhouette cut out of it until the photo arrives, and the photo itself in greyscale, multiplied onto the paper.

Overview

.art is the frame for every picture of an artist: the profile portrait in the poster, the headliner, the left column of a ticket, video thumbnails and the photo gallery. It always has the same 2px frame and print treatment, so real photos and placeholders sit together without looking broken.

  • Placeholder: until an artist uploads a photo, or while it loads, the halftone shows a silhouette: one singer at a mic, or a group.
  • Photo: an <img alt> inside .art is turned greyscale and multiplied onto the halftone paper.

Do not use .art for icons, logos or illustrations of the product. Avatars of people who are not artists (the booker’s initials) use the avatar.

Anatomy

  1. Dot screenA radial-gradient halftone, 0.6rem cells, --color-halftone-ink on --color-halftone-paper (ink on yellow for --yellow).
  2. Silhouette::before, a paper-coloured cut-out: one singer at a mic, or a row of heads with --group.
  3. Tag (optional).art__tag: an ink label pinned bottom-left, mono overline. Only “Headliner” today.
  4. Frame2px --color-border-strong, square corners, 12px inner padding for the tag. Paper-coloured on the stage.

Variants

Modifiers combine: a wide group shot, a square yellow portrait, a tilted dot screen. Choose the silhouette by what the photo will show, and use --tilt (a coarser screen) to tell neighbouring placeholders apart.

Headliner
Default (singer), --group, --tilt, --group --tilt, --yellow with .art__tag.
HTML
<div class="art" role="img" aria-label="Elijah Park seated on a stool with an acoustic guitar"></div>
<div class="art art--group" role="img" aria-label="Hosanna Collective, five musicians on a lit stage"></div>
<div class="art art--tilt" role="img" aria-label="Daniel and Ruth Okonkwo singing together at a piano"></div>
<div class="art art--yellow" role="img" aria-label="Abigail Mensah singing into a vintage microphone"><span class="art__tag">Headliner</span></div>

Media grid

.media-grid: wide art, one column; from 768px the first item spans the row and the rest sit three across. It usually holds video cards.

Sizes

Art has no fixed size: it fills its column and takes its height from an aspect ratio. Pick the ratio by where it goes.

Portrait 4:5 (default: poster, headliner), square 1:1 (--square: gallery), wide 16:9 (--wide: video). On a ticket the art fills the ticket’s height instead.

States

Art is not interactive on its own. When it is the face of a video button or sits in a ticket, the parent handles hover and focus. Its own states follow the image. On the stage the frame turns paper (.artist-poster > .art; drawn inline here).

Responsive behaviour

  • Art is fluid: it fills its grid cell and keeps its ratio. The dot cells stay 0.6rem, so a bigger frame shows more dots, not bigger ones.
  • .photo-grid: two columns, four from --layout-breakpoint-md (768px), 16px apart.
  • .media-grid: one column; from 768px the first item spans all three columns and the rest sit three across, 24px apart.
  • Photos use object-fit: cover; crop to keep faces in the upper third, since the frame can be portrait on a phone and wide in a video.

Theming

The dot screen reads --color-halftone-ink and --color-halftone-paper: mid-grey dots on bright paper in light; light grey dots on raised charcoal in dark. --yellow reads --color-accent and --color-border-on-accent, the same in both themes. The tag reads the inverse pair. The frame reads --color-border-strong, and on the stage --color-fg-on-stage.

In the dark theme a multiplied photo darkens onto charcoal paper, which keeps photos from glowing on the stage. To re-skin, override the two halftone tokens; never set a background on .art directly.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A placeholder that stands for a photo is <div class="art" role="img" aria-label="…">. A real photo is <img alt="…"> inside a plain .art. Art that only decorates (a repeated thumbnail next to the same name in a list) takes aria-hidden="true" and no label. Inside a <button>, such as a video card, children are presentational: the art’s label is not read, so the button’s own aria-label must carry what matters.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips art; it is never focusable on its own.

Focus

None of its own. A parent link or button draws the ring around the whole frame.

Labelling

Describe the photo, not the placeholder: who, doing what, where (“Abigail Mensah, eyes closed and one hand raised, singing into a vintage microphone”). Keep the same description when the photo arrives or fails, so the page reads the same either way. The tag text inside a role="img" element is part of the image and is not read; if it matters, put it in the label or nearby text.

The silhouette is decoration, so 1.4.11 does not require a ratio; the dots still clear 3:1 so the shape reads.

Motion

None. If photos fade in when loaded, use --duration-base, which drops to zero under reduced motion.

Touch

Not a target on its own.

Content

  • Labels: who, doing what, where, in one sentence without “Image of” or “Photo of”. Name the artist the first time; later photos in the same gallery can say “Abigail” only.
  • Groups: count and instruments (“five musicians on a lit stage with guitars and a drum kit”).
  • Pick the silhouette by the photo: --group for bands, choirs and duos with more than one face; default for solo artists.
  • Yellow is for the headliner and one gallery portrait at most. It is the top-of-the-bill colour.
  • Tags are one word, uppercase from CSS: “Headliner”.

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • .art--yellow dots and .art__tag read --palette-ink and --palette-paper. They now read --color-border-on-accent and the inverse pair, so the tag stays legible in the dark theme.
  • Art skeletons used inline aspect-ratio. They now use .skeleton--portrait and .skeleton--wide.
  • The grids used 1fr tracks, which let long content push columns wider. They now use minmax(0, 1fr).
  • New: .art > img defines the print treatment for real photos; the mocks only have placeholders.
