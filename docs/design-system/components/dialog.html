Dialog

A modal dialog stops the page to ask one question before something important happens: send this request, cancel this booking. It is a paper card on a dimmed page, with a dashed tear line under its title.

Overview

Use a dialog when the booker must decide before the product goes on, and the decision has consequences: sending a request an artist will see, cancelling a booking and its deposit, removing something they cannot get back. Keep everything else on the page.

  • Confirm: one question, a short summary of what will happen, a yellow primary action and a way back.
  • Destructive (.dialog--danger): a red rule across the top, the danger header icon (.dialog__icon--danger) and a red danger button that names what is lost. The title itself carries no icon.
  • Form: a few fields that belong to one small task, such as messaging an artist, so the booker does not lose their place on the page.

Do not use a dialog for success messages (use a toast), for errors on the page (use an alert), for help text (use a tooltip or inline copy) or for long flows such as the booking itself, which is a page. Never open a dialog the person did not ask for.

Anatomy

  1. Backdrop::backdrop in --color-bg-backdrop. The page behind is inert.
  2. Kicker (optional).dialog__kicker: the context, set in the overline style by the class itself (no .overline needed), “Abigail Mensah · Sat 14 Nov”.
  3. Title.dialog__title: an <h2> question in the display face, 28px. It names the dialog.
  4. CloseA ghost icon button labelled “Close”. Same as pressing Esc or the secondary action.
  5. Tear lineA 2px dashed rule under the header, the dialog’s ticket perforation.
  6. Body.dialog__body: what will happen, in one or two sentences. Scrolls if it must; the header and footer do not.
  7. Footer.dialog__footer: the way back first, then the primary (or danger) action last, right-aligned with 12px between, as in every dialog mock.

Variants

Static examples sit in .dialog-stage with .dialog--static so they can be read in place; the product always uses <dialog> and showModal().

Sizes

Medium (32rem, 512px) is the default. Small (.dialog--sm, 24rem) is for a one-line question. Large (.dialog--lg, 44rem) is for reading, such as the booking terms; its body scrolls. Every size leaves 16px of backdrop on each side from 576px and fills the screen below it (L2-099).

  • Open: fades in and rises 16px over --duration-slow; the backdrop dims the page.
  • Busy (aria-busy="true" on the dialog): the body drops to 60% opacity and ignores clicks; Esc and the way back are held until the request settles, so a request is never half-sent. The busy primary takes the button’s busy pattern: aria-busy="true" and aria-disabled="true" with the “Verb…” label, never native disabled. The exception is an upload dialog (add-video, add-photo, upload-check): while the bytes are sent it shows a progress bar and keeps “Cancel upload” enabled, so a person can stop a long upload.
  • Invalid: errors appear under their fields with aria-invalid and aria-describedby; focus moves to the error summary at the top of the body, which links to each field (as in Forms).
  • Failed: the dialog stays open with an alert at the top of the body that says what happened, that the input is safe, and what to do. Focus moves to “Try again”, with two exceptions. In destructive dialogs (withdraw a request, cancel a booking, an artist cancelling, decline a request, delete the account, turn off two-step sign-in, and the admin decisions: reject an application, suspend an artist, hide a review, issue a refund) it starts on the safe way back (“Keep request”, “Go back”, “Keep booking”, “Cancel”, “Keep my account”, “Keep it on”; “Cancel” in the reject, suspend and hide dialogs, “Go back” in the refund’s confirm step) so a second Enter cannot repeat the loss. When a card is declined (pay-deposit/failed, pay-balance/failed) focus goes to the decline alert and the primary reads “Pay with another card”, because the payment processor clears its card fields (L2-037 AC3).

Responsive behaviour

From 576px the dialog is centred with at least 16px of backdrop around it. Below 576px (XS) it fills the screen (L2-099): full width, the full 100dvh height, no frame or shadow. The header with the title and the close button stays at the top (padded by the top safe-area inset) and only the body scrolls, so the close button is always reachable without scrolling. The footer sticks to the bottom with the bottom safe-area inset, and its buttons stretch to equal widths. The navigation drawer and the anchored account menu keep their own sizes.

Theming

The dialog reads --color-bg-surface-raised and --color-fg-default for its paper, --color-border-strong for its frame and tear line, --shadow-4 for the hard 12px print shadow and --color-bg-backdrop for the dim layer. The danger variant adds --color-danger-solid for the top rule and --color-danger-icon and --color-danger-bg for the danger header icon (.dialog__icon--danger). One component token sets the width: --dialog-width (32rem; 24rem small; 44rem large).

In the dark theme the paper becomes a raised charcoal panel with a light frame, and the shadow turns yellow, like a stage light behind the card. The danger rule and icon switch to the lighter red of the dark palette, which keeps 3:1 against charcoal.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

APG Dialog (Modal) pattern, built on the native <dialog> element opened with showModal(). That gives the dialog role, a modal top layer, an inert page behind, Esc to close and focus return without extra code. Add aria-labelledby (the title) and, for confirmations, aria-describedby (the sentence that explains the consequence). The static documentation copies use role="dialog" aria-modal="true" on a <div> only so they can be shown in place.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the dialog’s controls only; the page behind cannot be reached.
EscCloses the dialog, the same as “Keep editing” or Close. Ignored while busy.
EnterActivates the focused button; in a form dialog, submits from a single-line field.
SpaceActivates the focused button.

Focus

On open, focus goes to: the primary action in a confirm dialog; the safe action (“Keep booking”) in a destructive one; the first field in a form; the start of the body (with tabindex="-1") when the body is long text. On close, focus returns to the button that opened it. If that button is gone (the booking was cancelled), move focus to the page’s <h1> or the list it was in.

Labelling

The title is a question or a task name, and it is the dialog’s accessible name. The close button is labelled “Close”. Busy and failed states are announced: the busy primary’s label changes to the verb in progress with an ellipsis (“Sending…”, “Posting…”, “Withdrawing…”) and it adds aria-busy="true" and aria-disabled="true", so a second press sends nothing (L2-108); the failure alert has role="alert".

Motion

The dialog fades in and rises 16px (dialog-in, --duration-slow, --ease-enter). Under prefers-reduced-motion the duration drops to near zero and it simply appears. The backdrop does not animate.

Touch

Footer buttons are 44px tall; on phones they stretch to share the full width and sit within thumb reach in the sticky footer. The close button is 36px, growing to 44px under a coarse pointer. Tapping the backdrop does not close a dialog that is busy or holds unsent input.

Content

  • Title: a direct question with the object: “Send request to Abigail?”, “Cancel your booking?”. Or a task name for forms: “Suggest another date”.
  • Body: what will happen and what it costs, in one or two sentences, with real numbers: “your $162.50 deposit comes back in full”.
  • Buttons: verbs that answer the title. “Send request” / “Keep editing”; “Cancel booking” / “Keep booking”. Never “OK” / “Cancel”, which is ambiguous when the action is itself a cancellation.
  • Failures follow the error formula: what happened, that the input is safe, what to do.
