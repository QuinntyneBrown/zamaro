Alert & banner

An alert sits where something happened and says what to do next: “We lost the signal. Try again.” A banner runs across the top of the page for news that affects every part of it.

Overview

Use an alert when one region of the page needs to explain itself: the lineup failed to load, a request was sent, a deposit is due. It stays in the layout until the problem is fixed or the booker dismisses it. Use a banner for news that affects the whole page or the whole site, such as planned maintenance or being offline.

  • Info (the default) adds context the booker would otherwise miss: travel is included within 120 km.
  • Success confirms something that lasts: “Request sent to Abigail Mensah.” For quick confirmations such as saving an artist, use a toast.
  • Warning flags something that needs attention soon but isn’t broken: a deposit due date, one date left.
  • Danger reports a failure and how to recover: “We lost the signal.” This is the tone the error mocks use.

Don’t use an alert for a problem with one field; use an inline message under the field. Don’t use it for a whole page that failed; use the error page composition, which wraps an alert in a stage poster.

Anatomy

  1. Icon28px (.icon--lg) in --alert-icon, aria-hidden. Info circle, check, or warning triangle for warning and danger.
  2. Title.alert__title: display face, 28px, uppercase. Says what happened in a few words.
  3. BodyOne or two sentences of body text: what is safe and what to do.
  4. Actions (optional).alert__actions: at most one primary and one secondary button. Sits under the text, aligned with it from 640px.
  5. Close (optional).alert__close on a ghost icon button, only on .alert--dismissible.
  6. Container2px rule in --alert-border, tinted fill --alert-bg, 24px padding, square corners.

Variants

The tone is a modifier on .alert. Plain .alert is info. Pick the tone from what happened, not from how loud you want it to be.

Dismissible

Add .alert--dismissible and a .alert__close button when the message is only for information and the booker has read it. Never make a danger alert dismissible while the problem is still there.

Request sent to Abigail Mensah

She usually replies within 2 days. We’ll email you at Riverside Community Church.

The close button is a 44px ghost icon button pinned to the top-right corner; the alert reserves room for it on the right.
HTML
<div class="alert alert--success alert--dismissible" role="status">
  …icon, title, body…
  <button class="btn btn--ghost btn--icon alert__close" type="button" aria-label="Dismiss: Request sent to Abigail Mensah">
    <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path d="M6 6l12 12M18 6 6 18"/></svg>
  </button>
</div>

Page banner

A .banner spans the full width directly under the top bar. It is a single line of small text with an optional small button. It defaults to warning; .banner--info and .banner--danger change the tone.

Zamaro is down for maintenance Sun 11 Oct, 1–3 am. Requests you send before then are safe.

Showing artists who drive to Burlington, ON.

Change location

You’re offline. Your search is kept, and the lineup will refresh when you reconnect.

Warning (default), info and danger banners. The text wraps; the button drops below it when space runs out.
HTML
<div class="banner banner--danger" role="status">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">…warning…</svg>
  <p class="banner__text">You’re offline. Your search is kept, and the lineup will refresh when you reconnect.</p>
  <button class="btn btn--sm" type="button">Try again</button>
</div>

Sizes

The default alert has 24px padding and a 28px title, for messages that explain a whole section. .alert--compact has 16px padding and a 22px title, for alerts inside a card, the booking stub or a dialog. Banners have one size.

The alert itself is static; its states live in its actions. Focus and hover are forced with data-state. A retry in progress sets aria-busy="true" on the button and keeps the alert in place until the result arrives.

  • Appearing: an alert that reports the result of an action (a failed retry, a sent request) is inserted into an element that already exists, so the live region announces it.
  • Retrying: the button goes busy; the alert text does not change until the result is known. On success the alert is removed and focus moves to the content that loaded.
  • Dismissed: the alert is removed from the DOM and focus moves to the next logical element, usually the section heading.

Responsive behaviour

  • Below 640px the actions span the full width under the icon and text (grid-column: 1 / -1). From 640px they line up with the text column.
  • Action buttons wrap onto a new line instead of shrinking. A label that is too long wraps to a balanced second line rather than overflowing, but that is a safety net: keep labels short enough for one line. The mock’s “Email the Zamaro team” just fits a full-width alert at 360px; inside anything narrower (a card, the stub, these examples) use “Email us”.
  • The alert fills its container. In the lineup it spans the container; in the booking stub it uses .alert--compact.
  • Banners pad with --layout-margin, so their text lines up with the page content at every breakpoint. The text takes the remaining width (flex: 1 1 16rem) and the button wraps below it on phones.

Theming

Alerts and banners read four component tokens that each tone sets from the status tokens: --alert-bg (tinted fill), --alert-fg (status text colour, reserved for status-coloured copy), --alert-border (the 2px rule) and --alert-icon. Title and body stay --color-fg-default, so body text always has full contrast. Warning uses --color-warning-icon for its rule because the warning border token is too pale to read as an edge.

/* A new tone needs only the four component tokens */
.alert--warning { --alert-bg: var(--color-warning-bg); --alert-fg: var(--color-warning-fg);
                  --alert-border: var(--color-warning-icon); --alert-icon: var(--color-warning-icon); }

In the dark theme the tinted fills become deep, low-chroma versions of each hue and the rule and icon lighten, so the status still reads on the charcoal stage. Buttons inside an alert keep their own tokens: primary stays yellow with an ink label.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Follows the APG Alert pattern. Use role="alert" only for a new, important message the booker must hear now, such as a failed load after they searched (as in the error mocks). Use role="status" for confirmations (“Request sent”) and for banners that appear while the page is open. An alert or banner that is part of the page when it loads needs no role. Never move focus into an alert just to announce it.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves through the actions, then the close button, in DOM order. The alert itself is not focusable.
Enter / SpaceActivates the focused action or the close button.
EscDoes nothing. Alerts are not overlays; only the close button dismisses them.

Focus

Actions use the standard ring: ink on newsprint, yellow in dark mode. After dismissing, move focus to the section heading or the next control so it isn’t lost on <body>. After a successful retry, focus stays on the page and the alert is removed.

Labelling

The title and body are read in order inside the live region. The icon is decorative (aria-hidden), because the title already states the tone. The close button names what it dismisses: “Dismiss: Request sent to Abigail Mensah”. A link-styled action names its destination (“Email the Zamaro team”, not “Contact”).

Motion

Alerts appear and disappear without animation, so the layout shift is a single step rather than a slide. Buttons inside keep their own --duration-fast lift, which reduced motion removes.

Touch

Action buttons are 44px (36px for .btn--sm in compact alerts and banners, which still clears the 24px minimum). The close button is a 44px square. Actions in a cluster are 12px apart.

Content

  • Title: what happened, in a few words, in sentence case in the source: “We lost the signal”, “Request sent to Abigail Mensah”, “Deposit due Fri 30 Oct”.
  • Body: what is safe and what to do, in one or two short sentences. “Your date, location and filters are kept.” is the reassurance the error mocks use.
  • For errors, say whose side it is on when you know: “It’s on our side, not yours.” Never blame the booker or show a raw error string.
  • Actions start with a verb and say what happens: “Try again”, “Pay deposit”, “Back to the lineup”. One primary action at most.
  • Banners are one sentence. Name the date and time for planned events: “Sun 11 Oct, 1–3 am”.
