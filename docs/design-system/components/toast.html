Toast

A small ink slip that rises in the corner to confirm something just happened: “Saved Luz Viva. Undo.” It leaves on its own, unless it reports an error.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stablerole="status" regionClass .toast .toast-region

Overview

Use a toast to confirm an action whose result isn’t visible where the booker is looking: saving an artist from the lineup (the count in the top bar changes far away), removing one, copying a booking code. Offer Undo when the action can be reversed.

  • Success confirms a change: “Saved Luz Viva”.
  • Info reports something the system did for the booker: “Search widened to 120 km”.
  • Warning means something needs attention soon: “Luz Viva deposit due. Pay your $225 deposit by Sat 10 Oct, 4:00 p.m. or the booking expires.” Losing the connection is not a toast: it is the danger “You’re offline” banner, and nothing is queued (L2-114)
  • Danger reports a failed background action and stays until the booker dismisses it or it succeeds on retry.

Don’t use a toast for anything the booker must read or act on to continue, such as a failed booking request; use an alert in place. Don’t use one for field problems; use an inline message. When a toast and a banner could both apply, see Notifications.

Anatomy

  1. Accent stripe4px left rule in --toast-accent: yellow for success and info, amber for warning, red for danger.
  2. Icon20px in --toast-accent, aria-hidden.
  3. Title.toast__title: 14px bold uppercase label. Says what happened.
  4. Body (optional).toast__body: one short sentence in --text-body-sm.
  5. Action (optional).toast__actions: one small outlined button, such as Undo or Retry.
  6. CloseA ghost icon button (.btn--ghost.btn--icon) named “Dismiss: …”.

Variants

Success, info and warning are ink slips (--color-bg-inverse) that differ only by stripe and icon. Danger is a red-tinted paper slip with a full rule, so an error never looks like a confirmation.

The booking, request, availability, saved and session toasts in the mocks use the full structure: a .toast__body column holding a small .toast__kind overline (“Accepted”, “Info”, “Heads up”), the .toast__title and a .toast__text sentence with the real date, time and amount. Sizes

Toasts have one size: 24rem wide from 576px, the full width minus 16px margins on phones.

States

A toast enters, waits, pauses while the booker is on it, and leaves. Pointer and focus states belong to its buttons and are forced here with data-state. data-state="leaving" is the real exit hook: script sets it, waits for the transition, then removes the node. Leaving is shown at half opacity in this table so you can see it; in the product it fades to 0 and slides 32px to the right.

Timing rules

RuleValue
Time on screen (success, info, warning, and toasts with an action such as Undo)5 seconds (L2-109), shown by the .toast__progress bar. Keep the text short enough to read in that time; anything longer or anything that must be acted on belongs in an alert or banner.
PauseThe timer pauses while the pointer is over the toast, while focus is inside it, and while the window is in the background, and resumes with the time that was left when they leave (L2-109).
Idle-session warningThe administrator’s “You’ll be signed out in 2 minutes.” warning with Stay signed in has no timer: it stays until the person acts or the session ends (L2-066, L2-109).
ErrorsDanger toasts have no timer and stay until dismissed (L2-109). They also leave when a retry succeeds (then a success toast replaces them).
StackingAt most three visible at once, newest first (on top, first in the region). Older ones wait in a queue and show as space frees; a queued error is never dropped (L2-109). Each toast keeps its own timer. Identical toasts merge (“Saved 3 artists”).
Enter / leaveRises 16px and fades in over --duration-slow (260ms, --ease-enter); leaves by fading and sliding 32px right over --duration-base (160ms, --ease-exit).

Responsive behaviour

  • Phones (below 576px): the region spans the screen with 16px side margins and sits 16px above the bottom edge plus env(safe-area-inset-bottom), so it clears the home indicator.
  • From 576px: the region is 24rem wide, 24px from the right and bottom edges (--space-6).
  • The region has pointer-events: none and each toast turns them back on, so the empty space around a toast never blocks clicks on the page.
  • A toast must never cover the focused element (WCAG 2.4.11). On pages with a sticky bottom bar, raise the region above it rather than overlapping it.
  • Long titles wrap; the close button stays top-right in its own column.

Theming

A toast is an inverse surface: --color-bg-inverse with --color-fg-inverse text. In the light theme that is a charcoal slip on newsprint; in the dark theme it flips to a paper slip on the stage, so it always stands out from the page. One component token, --toast-accent, sets the stripe and icon colour per tone. Buttons inside are re-mapped to transparent, inverse-outlined buttons that underline on hover instead of lifting:

.toast .btn { --btn-bg: transparent; --btn-fg: var(--color-fg-inverse); --btn-border: var(--color-fg-inverse);
              --btn-shadow-hover: none; --btn-transform-hover: none; --btn-bg-hover: var(--color-bg-inverse); }

The danger toast leaves the inverse surface for --color-danger-bg with a --color-danger-border rule and default text, and its buttons go back to ink outlines. The hard --shadow-2 is ink on light and yellow on dark.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The page has one .toast-region with role="status" (polite), present from page load, as in the mocks. Every toast, danger included, is inserted into it and read politely when the booker is idle (L2-102); a danger toast is told apart by its kind, title and icon, and by staying until dismissed, not by an assertive role. This follows WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages. Failures the booker must act on before continuing are alerts in the page, not toasts. A toast is not a dialog: it never traps or takes focus.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabReaches the toast’s action and close button in DOM order. The region sits at the end of <body>.
Enter / SpaceActivates Undo, Retry or Dismiss.
EscWhile focus is inside a toast, dismisses it and returns focus to the element that triggered it.

Focus

Showing a toast never moves focus. When focus is inside a toast the timer pauses. When the toast closes with focus inside it, focus returns to the control that caused it (the save toggle on Luz Viva’s ticket), never to <body>. Because a keyboard user may never reach a toast in time, every toast action must also be possible elsewhere: pressing the save toggle again is the same as Undo.

Labelling

The title and body are the announcement, so keep them complete: “Saved Luz Viva”, not “Saved”. The icon is decorative. The close button names the toast: “Dismiss: Saved Luz Viva”. An Undo button may be extended with aria-label="Undo save Luz Viva", keeping “Undo” first.

In the dark theme the yellow stripe and icon sit on a paper slip and fall below 3:1. They are decorative there: the title states the outcome and the slip itself contrasts strongly with the stage.

Motion

Entering rises 16px and fades; leaving fades and slides right. Under prefers-reduced-motion both durations drop to near zero, so toasts appear and disappear in place. Timing rules don’t change with reduced motion.

Touch

The close button is 44px; Undo and Retry are 36px (.btn--sm) with 8px between them. On phones the toast spans the width, so it is easy to reach with a thumb, and it never covers the bottom booking button: raise the region above sticky bars.

Content

  • Title: past tense, naming the object: “Saved Luz Viva”, “Removed Elijah Park”, “Copied No. ZAM-0114”. Four words or so.
  • Body (optional): one sentence that says where to find the result or what happens next.
  • Action: one verb. “Undo” for reversible changes, “Retry” for failures, “View” to jump to the result. Never “OK” or “Close”; the close button does that.
  • Errors follow the error formula in short: what failed, that nothing was lost, and Retry. “Couldn’t save Luz Viva. Your saved list hasn’t changed.”
  • Don’t celebrate (“Woohoo!”). A plain confirmation reads better at a church office desk.
