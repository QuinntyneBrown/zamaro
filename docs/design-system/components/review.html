Review

A review is what a church said after the service, in its own words: a quote under a yellow quote mark, the stars, and who said it.

Overview

Use reviews in the “What churches say” section of an artist profile, in a .reviews grid, newest first. Each review is one church’s experience with that artist, written after a completed booking.

  • For the overall score (“★ 4.9 · 38 churches”), use the rating component in the section head.
  • For a single pull quote inside another component, such as the headliner, use a plain paragraph with the reviewer muted after a dash, not a full review card.
  • When there are no reviews yet, keep the section and show a quiet empty state.
  • When the artist has replied (L2-061), the reply sits under its review as a .message whose meta line starts “Reply from {artist name}” (L2-018). Under each review a .cluster of small buttons holds what the viewer may do: Reply or Edit reply for the artist, Report for any signed-in user (L2-062).

Anatomy

  1. Quote markA “ on a yellow block, drawn by blockquote::before in --text-figure-lg.
  2. Quote<blockquote><p> in --text-body-lg. Titles of songs in <em>.
  3. Stars.stars: five characters in the stub font, role="img" with “5 out of 5 stars”.
  4. ReviewerName in the stub font, uppercase from CSS.
  5. Church and dateA <span> in the caption: church, town · month and year, muted, sentence case.
  6. CardPaper surface, 2px charcoal frame, 24px padding, 16px between parts.

Single review

One review on its own, for example the most recent one under a booking confirmation. Keep it on the page canvas or a paper surface: .review sets no text colour, so on the stage it would inherit paper-coloured text on its paper card.

Sizes

Reviews have one size; the grid sets their width. Keep quotes short instead of shrinking the type.

States

A review is static text with no interactive parts, so it has no hover, focus or disabled state. It changes with its data and the list’s loading state.

Both themes

  • Lower rating: empty stars are outline characters (☆), and the label says the number. There is no colour change: a 4-star review is not a warning.
  • Loading: review-shaped skeleton cards, aria-hidden, while the section has aria-busy="true".
  • No reviews yet: the section stays, with a quiet empty state and a way to become the first booking.

Responsive behaviour

  • .reviews is one column under --layout-breakpoint-md (768px) and two columns from 768px, 24px apart.
  • Cards in the same row stretch to the same height; the caption stays under the quote, not pinned to the bottom.
  • The church and date line wraps; the stars never wrap.

Theming

The card reads --color-bg-surface, --color-border-strong and --color-fg-default; the church and date line reads --color-fg-muted. The quote mark is a yellow fill (--color-accent) with ink type (--color-fg-on-accent) in both themes. The stars inherit the text colour, so they are ink on paper and paper on charcoal.

No component tokens.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Each review is a <figure> with a <blockquote> and a <figcaption>, which ties the quote to its source. The attribution is in the caption, not inside the blockquote, because it is not part of what was said. The reviews sit in a <ul role="list"> so the count is announced.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the review card; nothing in it is focusable. Stops on the Reply, Edit reply and Report buttons under it, in that order. Screen reader users move through reviews by list item or with the reading keys.

Focus

No focus style of its own.

Labelling

Reply, Edit reply and Report repeat on every review, so each carries visually hidden text naming the reviewer: “Report Tomi Oduya’s review”. The reply’s meta line starts “Reply from {artist name}” so it is never mistaken for the church’s words.

The star characters mean nothing when read one by one (“black star, black star…”), so .stars takes role="img" and an aria-label such as “4 out of 5 stars”. An aria-label on a plain <span> without a role is ignored by many screen readers. The quote mark is CSS content; some screen readers read it as “left double quotation mark”, which is accurate.

Content

  • Quote the church’s words, trimmed to one to three sentences about something that happened (“rehearsed with our volunteer band on Saturday”), not adjectives alone.
  • Song titles in <em>. Do not add quotation marks inside: the block already has one.
  • Reviewer: name with title as they gave it (“Rev. Janet Clarke”, “Pastor Femi Adebayo”).
  • Caption line: church, town · month and year: “St. Brendan’s Anglican, Oshawa · September 2026”.
  • Stars: whole numbers, filled ★ then empty ☆, five in total. The label states the same number.
  • Reply meta line: “Reply from {artist name} · {date}”. On the artist’s own Reviews page add “editable until {day date, time}” inside the 7-day window and “locked {day date}” after it; on the public profile show the month and year only.
