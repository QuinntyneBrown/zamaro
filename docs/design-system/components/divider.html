Divider

A printed rule between pieces of content. Thin when it just separates, strong when it closes a block, and dashed like a ticket’s tear line when what follows can be torn off: the price, the fine print, the actions.

WCAG 2.2 AAStatus: stableNative <hr>Class .divider

Overview

Use a divider when space alone does not show that two things are separate, for example inside a dense card or between a form and its fine print. Most separation in Zamaro comes from space (stacks and sections) and from the rules built into components: the dashed stub on a ticket, the dashed head of the booking ticket, the hairlines in a list. Reach for .divider only where no component already draws the line.

  • Hairline (default): 1px --color-border-default. Decorative.
  • Strong: 2px --color-border-strong, the same weight as card frames. Closes a block.
  • Perforated: 2px dashed, the tear line of a ticket. Use it above a price, totals or fine print that belong to what is above.
  • Vertical: a hairline between items in a row.
  • Label: hairlines either side of a short mono label, such as “Or pick a nearby date”.

Anatomy

  1. RuleA top border on an <hr>: 1px hairline, or 2px solid or dashed. Full width of its container.
  2. Label linesIn .divider--label, two flexible hairlines drawn by ::before and ::after.
  3. LabelMono 12px uppercase overline, muted, 12px from each line.
  4. Spacing24px (--space-6) above and below. Inside a stack, the stack gap sets it instead.

Sizes

Each variant has one weight (1px or 2px). Spacing comes from the 24px default margin or the surrounding stack.

States

Dividers are not interactive and have no hover, focus or disabled states. What changes is the surface under them; each variant is shown on the three paper surfaces it sits on.

On the stage (top bar, poster, footer) do not use these dividers: the stage separates with the 4px yellow rule (--border-width-poster, --color-accent) instead.

Responsive behaviour

  • Horizontal dividers are always the full width of their container.
  • A vertical divider in a row that wraps on phones ends up at a line start; switch the row to a stack below 640px and the vertical divider to a horizontal one, or drop it and rely on the gap.
  • The labelled divider’s text never wraps past one line at 360px; keep it under 30 characters.
  • On wide tickets the perforation turns vertical (the stub moves beside the body from 640px). That is the ticket’s own rule, not a .divider.

Theming

Hairlines read --color-border-default; strong and perforated rules read --color-border-strong; the label reads --color-fg-muted. All three follow the theme: on the stage theme the hairline becomes a dim grey line on charcoal and the strong rule a light grey one. There are no component tokens; to tint a divider inside a component, set border-color from a semantic token in that component, as the ticket does with --ticket-border.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A horizontal divider is a native <hr>, which has the separator role. A vertical divider is a <span role="separator" aria-orientation="vertical">. Neither is focusable; a focusable separator (a splitter) is a different APG pattern that Zamaro does not use. When a rule is pure decoration inside a component, draw it with a border, not an element.

The labelled divider is a <p>, not a separator: a separator’s content is not read, and “Or pick a nearby date” must be.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips dividers. They are never focusable.

Focus

Not focusable. Keep 24px between a divider and the next control so its focus ring never overlaps the rule.

Labelling

Screen readers announce an <hr> as a separator. Use it between groups of content, not between every list item; lists already announce their items.

Contrast

Hairlines carry no information on their own, so WCAG 1.4.11 does not apply. When a rule does carry meaning (the perforation that says “this part is the payment”), use the strong or perforated variant, which clears 3:1.

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. Dividers between targets must not shrink the 8px minimum gap between them.

Content

  • Labelled dividers name the alternative that follows, in a few words: “Or pick a nearby date”, “Or send your own list”.
  • Sentence case in the source; the overline style uppercases it.
  • Do not use a divider to replace a heading. If the next block needs a name, give it a heading.

Sources

The mocks use no standalone <hr>; the divider standardises rules that the mocks drew inside components:

  • pages/discover/default: the hairline between page sections, the dashed stub on every ticket, the dashed title rule of the booking bar
  • pages/artist/default: the dashed head of the booking ticket (“Book Abigail · No. ZAM-0114”), the hairlines between setlist songs and tour dates
  • pages/discover/empty: nearby dates offered as an alternative (“Try a nearby date, or widen the radius”), where a labelled divider can introduce the date list

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • No standalone divider existed, so “Or pick a nearby date” in the empty state had to be a heading or nothing. .divider--label fills that gap. All rules now share two widths (--border-width-hairline, --border-width-thick) and two colours (--color-border-default, --color-border-strong).
  • The ticket notch, which sits on the perforation, always painted --color-bg-canvas; it now reads --ticket-notch-bg so the tear line works on any surface.
