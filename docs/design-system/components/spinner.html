Spinner

A turning ink ring for short waits with no measurable end. It always comes with words, visible or not, that say what Zamaro is doing: “Checking calendars…”.

Overview

Use a spinner when something will finish in a few seconds and you can’t say how far along it is: checking who is free, sending a request, saving a message. Put it as close as possible to what the booker did.

  • In a button: set aria-busy="true" on the button. It draws its own spinner after the label, so don’t add a .spinner inside it.
  • Inline with text (.spinner-inline): a small ring and a status line, such as “Checking calendars…” under a field or above a list.
  • On its own (.spinner): a ring in an area that is waiting for content, with visually hidden text.

If the content has a known shape (the lineup, a profile), use a skeleton instead, so the page doesn’t jump when it arrives. If the wait is longer than about 10 seconds or measurable (an upload), use a progress bar.

Anatomy

  1. RingA circle drawn with a currentColor rule: 2px for sm and md, 4px (--border-width-poster) for lg. Square ends are impossible on a circle, so this is the one round shape in the system besides the save toggle.
  2. GapOne transparent quarter that shows the turn. One turn takes --duration-loop (900ms), linear.
  3. Status textVisible in .spinner-inline (mono, 14px, uppercase); visually hidden for a lone spinner. This is what assistive tech reads.

Sizes

Match the spinner to the text beside it. Small sits with 12–14px text and inside dense rows; medium with 16px body text; large stands alone in an area that is waiting, such as a video that is processing.

States

A spinner has one state: turning. What changes is where it sits and what happens around it. Busy controls use aria-busy="true", which also ignores further clicks.

Timing

  • Delay: wait 400ms before showing a spinner. Most saves finish sooner, and a ring that flashes for 100ms reads as a glitch.
  • Minimum: once shown, keep it for at least 600ms so the change of state can be seen and read.
  • Long waits: after 10 seconds, add a sentence (“Still checking. Some artists’ calendars are slow to answer.”) or switch to a progress bar if the task can report one.
  • Done: replace the spinner with the result or a short confirmation; don’t leave an empty gap.

Responsive behaviour

  • Spinners don’t change size with the breakpoint; they follow the text they sit with.
  • .spinner-inline is an inline flex row; long status text wraps under itself and the ring stays at the start of the first line.
  • A busy button keeps its width: the label may change (“Show the lineup” → “Checking calendars…”), but a block button in the stub stays full width and a lone button never shrinks. If the busy label is longer, check it fits at 360px.

Theming

The spinner has no component tokens and no colour of its own: it is drawn in currentColor with a transparent quarter. Set the colour on the parent. That is why it reads ink on newsprint, paper on the stage and ink on a yellow primary button without extra rules. The ring width comes from --border-width-thick (sm, md) and --border-width-poster (lg); the turn from --duration-loop.

.spinner { border: var(--border-width-thick) solid currentColor; border-right-color: transparent;
           border-radius: var(--radius-full); animation: spin var(--duration-loop) linear infinite; }

Dark theme needs nothing: currentColor becomes the light text colour.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The ring is decorative (aria-hidden="true"). The words are the status: put them in a role="status" element that exists before the wait starts, so “Checking calendars…” is announced politely. For a lone spinner the words are visually hidden. A busy control or region takes aria-busy="true". There is no APG widget pattern; the rule is WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages. Never put role="status" on a heading: it replaces the heading role.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabThe spinner is not focusable. A busy button stays focusable so focus isn’t lost; activating it again does nothing until it is done.

Focus

Keep focus on the control that started the wait. Don’t set disabled on a busy submit button: a disabled button drops focus to <body>. Use aria-busy, which keeps it focusable and ignores clicks.

Labelling

Say what is happening with a present-tense verb and an ellipsis: “Checking calendars…”, “Loading Abigail’s videos”. A busy button keeps its accessible name; the page’s status region carries the progress message.

Motion

The ring turns once every --duration-loop (900ms). It keeps turning under prefers-reduced-motion, because it is a status indicator and a still ring would look frozen; the token is deliberately left out of the reduced-motion overrides. It is a small, slow, non-flashing rotation (well under three flashes a second).

Touch

Not a target. A busy button keeps its full target size; repeat taps are ignored (pointer-events: none) so a double tap can’t send a request twice.

Content

  • Present tense and an ellipsis: “Checking calendars…”, “Sending request…”, “Saving…”.
  • Name what you’re doing for the booker, not the system step: “Finding who’s free on Saturday 14 November…”, not “Fetching results”.
  • A busy button may change to a present-tense version of its label, as long as it isn’t much longer: “Show the lineup” → “Checking calendars…”.
  • Never “Please wait” on its own.
