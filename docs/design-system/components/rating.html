Rating

A rating tells Naomi how other churches found an artist: ★★★★★ 4.9 from 38 churches. It is printed text, read the same by eyes and screen readers.

Overview

Use the rating wherever an artist’s reputation helps a booker decide: the profile poster, the headliner, a ticket and the reviews heading. Pair it with the number of churches, because 4.9 from 38 churches means more than 5.0 from one.

  • Full (.rating: stars, score, count) on the profile and the reviews heading.
  • Compact (score and count, no stars) in tight places: ticket numbers and list meta.
  • Inline (one role="img" span: a single ★, the score and the count, “★ 4.9 · 38 churches”) inside a line of facts: the profile poster, the headliner meta, ticket numbers, the reviews heading, the dashboard and admin rows. This is the form every mock uses; the .rating classes above are ready but not yet in the mocks.
  • Stars only (.stars) for a single review, where the score is one church’s whole-star rating.

Rating input: a radio group, not clickable stars

The rating is display-only. Churches give their score once, after the event, in the write-review dialog (L2-059), and that input is a native radio group of five .choice--stub cards (“Star rating” on the radio group page): each shows the stars as aria-hidden text, says “{n} out of 5 stars” in visually hidden text and names the score in a word, Unforgettable, Great, Good, Fair or Poor. Star widgets commonly fail the basics: hover-only previews, no arrow-key support, 16px targets, and no way to say what three stars means. A radio group gives every option a word, a large target and keyboard support for free, following the APG Radio Group pattern. Do not build a clickable row of .stars.

Anatomy

  1. Stars.stars: five glyphs, ★ filled and ☆ empty, in the mono stub face with wide tracking. In the full and compact forms the whole .rating is one role="img" named “Rated 4.9 out of 5 by 38 churches” (L2-102); on its own, for one review, .stars takes role="img" and “4 out of 5 stars”.
  2. Score.rating__score: one decimal in the display figure face, 24px. Hidden from screen readers because the stars’ label already says it.
  3. Count.rating__count: how many churches rated, muted stub text.

Sizes

The rating has one size: stars and count at 14px, the score at the 24px figure size. Stars-only follows the text it sits in.

States

The rating is read-only, so it has no hover, focus or pressed state. It has data states: rated, still loading, and not rated yet.

Responsive behaviour

  • The rating is an inline row that wraps between its parts, never inside the stars. At 360px the full rating (about 15rem) fits on one line.
  • On ticket cards at every width put the score and the count in the ticket number line: “No. 02 · ★ 4.6 · 17 churches”, so every card shows its review count (L2-006).
  • The score never scales with the viewport.

Theming

The rating has no component tokens. Stars and score use the current text colour (--color-fg-default on paper), the count uses --color-fg-muted. On the stage the stars take --color-accent-on-stage and the count --color-fg-on-stage-muted; today that needs the two overrides listed under Code, which the stage version above applies inline.

Stars are text, not icons, so they print, copy and scale with the type, and they follow forced-colours mode.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Static text. An artist’s rating is one role="img" whose aria-label says it in words: “Rated 4.9 out of 5 by 38 churches” (L2-102). Without it, screen readers read “black star, black star…” five times, then the score and the count. The rating itself is not interactive; the score is entered with a radio group (see Overview and the radio group page), following the APG Radio Group.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the rating; it is never focusable. A link to the reviews (“Read 38 reviews”) sits next to it when one is needed.

Focus

No focus. Don’t make the stars a link; link the words instead.

Labelling

The full and compact ratings are read as one phrase, “Rated 4.9 out of 5 by 38 churches” (L2-102): the label is on the .rating wrapper with role="img", so the stars, score and count inside are not read again. The inline form in the mocks does the same with one role="img" span. A single review’s stars say that church’s whole-star rating (“4 out of 5 stars”).

Content

  • Score: one decimal, always (“5.0”, “4.9”). Glyphs round to the nearest whole star, halves up: 4.9 and 4.6 show five, 4.4 shows four. The score gives the precision.
  • Always five glyphs: filled ★ then empty ☆. No half-star glyphs; they don’t exist in most fonts.
  • Count names who rated: “38 churches”, not “38 reviews” or “(38)”. In the compact form, “· 38” is enough when the context already says churches.
  • An artist’s rating is labelled “Rated {score} out of 5 by {n} churches” (L2-102), on the full, compact and inline forms alike; “Rated {score} out of 5” only where the count is shown elsewhere. A single review’s .stars say “{n} out of 5 stars”.
  • Separate the score and the count with a middle dot: “★ 4.9 · 38 churches”, never brackets.
  • Unrated artists: “New · No reviews yet” on the profile, “New” on a ticket.

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • .review figcaption span also restyled the stars span. It is now scoped to non-star spans, so .stars keeps its own type in reviews.
  • Ratings were typed four different ways (★ 4.9, ★★★★★ 4.9 · 38 reviews, ★ 4.9 (38 churches), No. 02 · ★ 4.8). The mocks now use one labelled inline form, “★ {score} · {n} churches”, and one count noun: churches. The .rating full and compact classes are ready for the product but no mock uses them yet.
  • Star strings carried aria-label without a role, and single typed ★ glyphs had no label at all. Stars are now role="img" with “{score} out of 5 stars”, and inline ratings with “Rated {score} out of 5 by {n} churches”.
